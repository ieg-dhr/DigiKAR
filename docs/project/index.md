# The DigiKAR Project

The geohumanities project DigiKAR (Digitale Kartenwerkstatt Altes Reich - Digital Map Lab Holy Roman Empire) was a collaboration between the Leibniz Institute for European History in Mainz (IEG), the Leibniz Institute for Regional Geography in Leipzig (IfL), the Leibniz Institute for East and Southeast European Studies in Regensburg (IOS), Johannes Gutenberg University Mainz (JGU), and the École des Hautes Études en Sciences Sociales in Paris, France (EHESS).  
  
The project addressed issues of fragmentation, interconnection, plurality, and competition among spatial structures in the early modern Holy Roman Empire. Based on case studies examining the Electorates of Mainz and Saxony, the Digital Map Lab developed alternative approaches to capturing, processing, and representing ambiguous spatial configurations and practices. Thus, DigiKAR has made a general contribution to historical research on the Holy Roman Empire as a space of shared and overlapping sovereignty. It also developed innovative concepts and best practices for collecting, modeling, and visualizing spatial historical data that can be transferred to other research areas.

The project was funded for three years (2021–2024), with an extension to 2025, by the "Leibniz Cooperative Excellence" program of the Leibniz Association.

This documentation gives an insight into data and data queries in the DigiKAR project. The work package "Electoral Saxony" focuses on spatial structuring via rulers' rights, while the work package "Electoral Mainz" records the mobility of officials via biographical events. Explanations of the data structure and the research questions are documented exclusively in English, the short descriptions of the scripts and their use cases are available in German, English and French. In addition to the short descriptions, blogposts and videos (German and/or English) explaining the processes are linked.

## Further information

If you want to find out more about DigiKAR, you are welcome to visit [our website](https://digikar.eu/). The project has ended in 2025. However, please feel free to still get in touch with the Leibniz Institute of European History with any questions you may have. Please contact the team members at the institute's DH Lab [digital@ieg-mainz.de](mailto:digital@ieg-mainz.de). The DH Lab coordinates the development and expansion of digital research infrastructures operated or used by the IEG and manages the digital research collections and services offered by IEG digital.   
You are also welcome to download the [full conference presentation](https://insulae.hypotheses.org/files/2022/08/Blog_VisualizationAsExploration.pdf), containing the displayed slides.
